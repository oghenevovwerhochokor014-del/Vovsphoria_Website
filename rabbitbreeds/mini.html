All About the Mini Rex Rabbit

The Mini Rex is a small domestic rabbit breed famous for its unusually soft, dense, velvet-like coat. It combines the distinctive Rex fur with a compact body. Mini Rex rabbits are popular as companion and show rabbits and are often curious, playful, and friendly when properly socialized.

Origin

The Mini Rex was developed in the United States during the 1980s. Its development is closely associated with American rabbit breeder Monna Berryhill, who worked with small Rex-type rabbits to establish a miniature breed with the characteristic Rex coat.

The Rex coat is caused by a genetic variation that changes the length and structure of the rabbit's fur. Breeders sought to preserve this distinctive texture while developing a smaller, compact rabbit.

The Mini Rex became established as a separate breed and gained popularity among rabbit enthusiasts because of its small size, variety of colors, and plush coat.

Appearance

The Mini Rex has a compact, well-rounded body, upright ears, and a short neck. Its overall appearance is balanced and sturdy despite its relatively small size.

Its most distinctive feature is its Rex coat. Unlike the typical rabbit coat, the guard hairs are shortened so that they are approximately the same length as the undercoat. This creates a dense, upright coat with an exceptionally soft, plush texture often compared to velvet.

Mini Rex rabbits come in many colors and patterns, including black, blue, chocolate, white, red, lilac, otter, and broken patterns. Recognized varieties depend on the relevant rabbit organization's standards.

Adult Mini Rex rabbits commonly weigh approximately 1.4 to 2 kg, making them considerably smaller than the standard Rex rabbit.

Personality and Temperament

Mini Rex rabbits are often described as curious, gentle, and playful. Individual personalities vary, but many enjoy exploring their surroundings and interacting with familiar people.

They benefit from regular, gentle interaction and a calm environment. Some may enjoy being petted once they feel comfortable, while others prefer to interact without being picked up.

Like other rabbits, Mini Rex rabbits can become frightened by loud noises, sudden movements, or rough handling. They should be approached calmly, and their bodies should be properly supported whenever handling is necessary.

Diet

A balanced diet is essential for maintaining a Mini Rex rabbit's digestive health, dental health, and overall condition.

A suitable diet should include:

  • Unlimited grass hay: Good-quality hay should be available at all times to support digestion and natural tooth wear.
  • Fresh water: Clean drinking water must always be available.
  • Leafy greens: Suitable rabbit-safe greens provide variety and additional nutrients.
  • Rabbit pellets: A measured amount of quality pellets helps provide essential nutrients.
  • Occasional treats: Small portions of suitable fruit can be offered occasionally.

New foods should be introduced gradually. Excessive pellets and sugary treats can contribute to digestive problems and unhealthy weight gain. The diet should be adjusted to the rabbit's age, condition, and individual needs.

Housing and Environment

Mini Rex rabbits need a spacious, secure living area where they can stand upright, stretch fully, hop, and explore. A small cage alone does not provide enough room for their daily exercise and natural behaviours.

Their enclosure should include comfortable, non-slip flooring, a clean resting area, hiding places, and access to a larger exercise space. Soft, dry flooring is especially important because Mini Rex rabbits can be prone to sore hocks.

Wire flooring should be avoided because it can place pressure on their feet and contribute to injuries. The enclosure should be kept clean, dry, and well ventilated.

Mini Rex rabbits are sensitive to excessive heat. They need shade, good airflow, fresh water, and a comfortably cool environment during hot weather. Their living space should also be protected from predators, toxic plants, and electrical cables.

Grooming

Mini Rex rabbits have a distinctive coat that generally requires relatively little grooming. Occasional gentle brushing can help remove loose fur and keep the coat clean.

Their fur is different from that of most other rabbit breeds, so aggressive brushing or grooming tools that pull at the coat should be avoided. Excessive grooming can damage the delicate fur.

During shedding periods, monitor the rabbit's coat and remove loose hair gently. Rabbits groom themselves and may swallow loose fur, so regular observation and a hay-rich diet are important.

Their nails should be checked and trimmed when necessary. Their teeth, skin, feet, and ears should also be monitored for signs of health problems. Routine bathing is not recommended.

Health

Mini Rex rabbits can live healthy lives with appropriate care. However, they can develop health problems common to domestic rabbits, and their coat structure makes foot care particularly important.

Potential health concerns include:

  • Sore hocks: Mini Rex rabbits may be more susceptible to sore hocks because their feet have less protective fur than those of many other breeds.
  • Dental problems: Tooth abnormalities can interfere with normal tooth wear and eating.
  • Digestive problems: Gastrointestinal stasis is a serious condition in which normal digestive movement slows down or stops.
  • Obesity: Excessive feeding and insufficient exercise can lead to unhealthy weight gain.
  • Respiratory infections: These may cause sneezing, nasal discharge, or breathing difficulties.
  • Heat stress: High temperatures can be dangerous for rabbits, particularly in poorly ventilated environments.

Appropriate flooring, unlimited hay, fresh water, regular exercise, and routine veterinary check-ups help support good health. A rabbit that stops eating or producing droppings, becomes unusually inactive, or shows signs of pain needs prompt veterinary attention.

Exercise and Enrichment

Mini Rex rabbits need daily opportunities to exercise outside their resting area. Regular movement helps maintain healthy muscles, supports normal behaviour, and reduces the risk of obesity.

Tunnels, cardboard boxes, rabbit-safe chew toys, and digging boxes can provide mental stimulation and encourage natural behaviours such as exploring, hiding, and chewing.

Their exercise area should be secure and free from electrical cables, toxic plants, and other hazards. Outdoor exercise requires protection from predators, extreme weather, and escape.

As a Companion Rabbit

Mini Rex rabbits can make rewarding companions for people who understand rabbit behaviour and can provide appropriate housing, nutrition, and veterinary care. Their small size and unusual coat make them appealing to many rabbit enthusiasts.

They need daily care, opportunities to exercise, and a safe environment. Although their coat is relatively easy to maintain, their feet should be monitored regularly to detect signs of irritation or soreness.

Rabbits are social animals and may benefit from living with a compatible rabbit companion. Introductions should be managed carefully, and neutering or spaying should be discussed with a rabbit-experienced veterinarian.

Lifespan

Mini Rex rabbits commonly live for around 5 to 8 years, although some may live longer with good care. Genetics, diet, housing, exercise, and access to appropriate veterinary treatment can all influence their lifespan.
