All About the Bombay Cat

The Bombay is a sleek cat breed known for its glossy black coat, rounded features, muscular body, and copper or gold eyes. Bombay cats are affectionate, playful, intelligent, and often enjoy spending time with their human companions.

Origin

The Bombay cat was developed in the United States during the 1950s by breeder Nikki Horner.

Horner wanted to create a domestic cat that resembled a small black panther. She worked toward this goal by crossing black American Shorthair cats with Burmese cats.

The resulting breed became known as the Bombay, named after the Indian city of Bombay, now called Mumbai. Despite its name, the breed was developed in the United States.

Appearance

Bombay cats have a distinctive appearance, combining a sleek coat with a compact, muscular body.

  • Size: Medium
  • Body: Muscular and compact
  • Legs: Medium-length and sturdy
  • Ears: Medium-sized with rounded tips
  • Eyes: Rounded, usually copper or gold
  • Tail: Medium-length
  • Coat: Short, smooth, and glossy
  • Color: Solid black

Their close-fitting black coat often has a polished, shiny appearance. Their rounded head and expressive eyes contribute to their distinctive look.

Personality

Bombay cats are generally affectionate, playful, curious, and intelligent. Many enjoy being around people and may follow their owners from room to room.

They often enjoy interactive play and learning routines. Although they can be energetic, they also appreciate relaxing near their families.

Individual personalities vary, but many Bombay cats thrive when they receive regular attention and companionship.

Intelligence and Training

Bombay cats are intelligent and curious, which can make them responsive to reward-based training.

With patience and positive reinforcement, they may learn simple cues, household routines, and interactive games. Puzzle feeders and treat-searching activities can also provide mental stimulation.

Exercise

Bombay cats benefit from regular play and opportunities to climb, explore, and investigate their surroundings.

Activities they may enjoy include:

  • Chasing toys
  • Playing with balls
  • Interactive games with their owners
  • Climbing cat trees
  • Exploring safe indoor spaces
  • Using puzzle toys
  • Practising short training exercises

Providing scratching posts, climbing areas, and a variety of toys can help keep them active and entertained.

Diet

Bombay cats need a complete and balanced diet appropriate for their age, size, health, and activity level.

Their food portions should suit their individual needs to help maintain a healthy body condition. Treats should be offered in moderation.

Fresh, clean water should always be available. A veterinarian can help recommend a suitable diet if a cat has specific nutritional needs.

Grooming

Bombay cats have a short, close-fitting coat that is relatively easy to maintain.

  • Brush the coat regularly to remove loose hair.
  • Trim the nails when necessary.
  • Check the ears and clean them when appropriate.
  • Brush the teeth regularly using cat-safe products.
  • Keep the litter tray clean.

Their short coat generally does not require extensive grooming, although regular care helps maintain their coat and overall wellbeing.

Health

Bombay cats can live healthy lives when provided with appropriate care. However, they may be affected by health conditions also seen in related breeds and domestic cats.

Potential health concerns include:

  • Heart conditions, including hypertrophic cardiomyopathy
  • Obesity
  • Dental disease
  • Respiratory or nasal concerns in some cats
  • Inherited conditions associated with their ancestry

These problems are not inevitable in every Bombay cat. Regular veterinary checkups, appropriate nutrition, dental care, and maintaining a healthy activity level can help support their wellbeing.

Responsible breeders may also screen breeding cats for relevant inherited health concerns.

As a Family Cat

Bombay cats can make affectionate and playful companions for families who can provide them with attention, enrichment, and proper care.

Many enjoy human interaction and form close bonds with their owners. Because they are often sociable, some may become bored when left alone for long periods without stimulation.

They may live comfortably with children and other compatible pets when introductions are handled carefully. Children should be taught to treat cats gently and respect their need for space.

Lifespan

Bombay cats commonly live around 12–16 years, although some may live longer or shorter depending on genetics, lifestyle, and overall healthcare.

Fun Facts

  • The Bombay breed was developed in the United States.
  • The breed's development began during the 1950s.
  • Breeder Nikki Horner wanted a domestic cat resembling a small black panther.
  • The breed was developed using Burmese and black American Shorthair cats.
  • Bombay cats are known for their glossy, solid-black coats.
  • Their eyes are commonly copper or gold in color.
  • Despite its name, the Bombay breed did not originate in India.
  • Many Bombay cats enjoy companionship and interactive play.
