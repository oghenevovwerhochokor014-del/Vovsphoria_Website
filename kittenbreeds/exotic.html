All About the Exotic Shorthair

The Exotic Shorthair is a charming cat breed known for its round face, large eyes, short plush coat, and calm personality. Often described as a short-haired version of the Persian, Exotic Shorthairs are affectionate, playful, and generally easygoing.

Origin

The Exotic Shorthair was developed in the United States during the 1950s and 1960s.

Breeders crossed Persian cats with American Shorthairs and other short-haired cats to develop a cat with the Persian's rounded features and a short, dense coat.

Over time, breeders refined the breed's appearance and temperament. The Exotic Shorthair was eventually recognized as a distinct breed by major cat organizations.

Appearance

Exotic Shorthairs have a compact, sturdy, and rounded appearance.

  • Size: Medium
  • Body: Compact, muscular, and sturdy
  • Legs: Short and strong
  • Ears: Small, rounded, and set apart
  • Eyes: Large, round, and expressive
  • Tail: Short and thick
  • Coat: Short, dense, and plush
  • Colors: Many colors and patterns, including white, black, blue, cream, red, tabby, tortoiseshell, and color-point patterns

Their round head, short muzzle, full cheeks, and large eyes give them their distinctive appearance.

Personality

Exotic Shorthairs are generally calm, affectionate, playful, gentle, and friendly.

They often enjoy spending time with their owners and may follow family members around the home. However, their personalities vary, and some are more independent than others.

They enjoy short periods of play as well as relaxing in comfortable places around the house.

Exercise

Exotic Shorthairs usually have moderate energy levels and benefit from regular play and activity.

They enjoy activities such as:

  • Chasing toys
  • Playing with balls
  • Interactive games
  • Climbing onto suitable platforms
  • Exploring safe indoor spaces
  • Using puzzle toys
  • Short play sessions with their owners

Regular activity helps maintain muscle strength and supports a healthy body condition. Monitoring food portions and encouraging play can help reduce the risk of excess weight gain.

Diet

Exotic Shorthairs need a complete and balanced diet appropriate for their age, size, health, and activity level.

Their food portions should be adjusted to their individual needs because they may gain excess weight if they regularly consume more energy than they use.

Fresh, clean water should always be available. A veterinarian can help determine an appropriate feeding plan.

Grooming

Although the Exotic Shorthair has a short coat, its dense fur benefits from regular grooming to remove loose hair and maintain its condition.

  • Brush the coat regularly.
  • Gently clean around the eyes when necessary.
  • Trim the nails regularly.
  • Check the ears and clean them when needed.
  • Maintain regular dental care.
  • Keep the litter tray clean.

Their relatively flat facial structure can contribute to tear overflow and staining around the eyes. Gentle cleaning can help keep the area comfortable, but persistent discharge or irritation should be checked by a veterinarian.

Health

Exotic Shorthairs may be susceptible to certain health conditions, including some associated with their flat facial structure.

Potential health concerns include:

  • Breathing difficulties: Their shortened muzzle can make breathing less comfortable, particularly in hot weather or during exertion.
  • Eye problems: Their facial structure can contribute to excessive tearing and eye irritation.
  • Dental problems: Their shortened jaws may contribute to dental alignment issues.
  • Polycystic kidney disease: An inherited condition associated with Persian ancestry.
  • Heart disease: Certain heart conditions may occur in the breed.
  • Obesity: Their sturdy build and relatively moderate activity levels make weight management important.

Regular veterinary checkups and appropriate health screening can help identify potential problems early. Responsible breeders should screen for relevant inherited conditions.

Intelligence and Training

Exotic Shorthairs are curious and can learn through gentle, reward-based training.

They may learn to respond to their names, use interactive toys, or perform simple tricks when training is patient and consistent.

Short, enjoyable sessions provide mental stimulation while helping strengthen their bond with their owners.

As a Family Cat

Exotic Shorthairs can make affectionate and relaxed family companions.

They often enjoy human company and may be happy to relax near their family members.

They benefit from a comfortable home, regular interaction, gentle play, and a predictable routine.

Children should be taught to handle cats gently and respect their need for space and rest. Their facial structure also means owners should pay attention to breathing comfort, especially in warm conditions.

Lifespan

Exotic Shorthairs commonly live around 12–15 years, although individual cats may live longer or shorter lives.

Genetics, nutrition, weight management, living conditions, and veterinary care can all influence their lifespan.

Fun Facts

  • The Exotic Shorthair was developed in the United States.
  • The breed was developed using Persian cats and short-haired breeds.
  • It is often called the short-haired Persian.
  • Its dense coat has a soft, plush texture.
  • Its large, round eyes and full cheeks give it a distinctive appearance.
  • Exotic Shorthairs are generally relaxed but still enjoy interactive play.
  • Their short coat usually requires less grooming than a Persian's long coat.
