All About the Devon Rex

The Devon Rex is a distinctive cat breed known for its large ears, expressive eyes, short wavy coat, slender body, and playful personality. These cats are intelligent, curious, affectionate, and highly social.

Origin

The Devon Rex originated in Devon, England, during the late 1950s.

The breed developed from a curly-coated kitten named Kirlee, who was discovered near an abandoned tin mine.

Breeders initially thought Kirlee might be related to the Cornish Rex. However, genetic differences revealed that the two breeds had different mutations responsible for their curly coats. The Devon Rex was subsequently developed as a separate breed.

Appearance

Devon Rex cats have an unusual and easily recognizable appearance.

  • Size: Small to medium
  • Body: Slender, muscular, and athletic
  • Legs: Long and slender
  • Ears: Very large and set low on the head
  • Eyes: Large and expressive
  • Tail: Long and slender
  • Coat: Short, fine, soft, and wavy
  • Colors: Many colors and patterns, including black, white, cream, red, blue, tabby, and tortoiseshell

Their large ears, wide eyes, prominent cheekbones, and short wavy coat give them a distinctive appearance.

Personality

Devon Rex cats are generally intelligent, playful, curious, affectionate, and sociable.

They often enjoy staying close to their owners and may follow family members around the house.

Their curious nature means they enjoy exploring their surroundings, investigating objects, and participating in household activities.

Exercise

Devon Rex cats are active and energetic, so they benefit from regular play and mental stimulation.

They enjoy activities such as:

  • Chasing toys
  • Playing fetch
  • Climbing
  • Jumping
  • Interactive games
  • Using puzzle toys
  • Exploring safe indoor spaces
  • Playing with their owners

Cat trees, climbing platforms, and interactive toys can provide exercise and help keep them entertained.

Diet

Devon Rex cats need a complete and balanced diet appropriate for their age, size, health, and activity level.

Food portions should be adjusted to their individual needs to help them maintain a healthy body condition.

Fresh, clean water should always be available.

Grooming

The Devon Rex has a short, fine coat that generally requires little brushing. Its delicate coat should be handled gently to avoid damaging the hair.

  • Groom the coat gently and avoid excessive brushing.
  • Bathe when necessary and appropriate.
  • Trim the nails regularly.
  • Check the ears regularly and clean them when needed.
  • Maintain regular dental care.
  • Keep the litter tray clean.

Their large ears may accumulate wax, so regular checks are helpful. Their relatively thin coat can also make them sensitive to cold temperatures, so provide a warm, comfortable resting place.

Health

Devon Rex cats can live healthy lives, but the breed may be susceptible to certain health conditions.

Potential health concerns include:

  • Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, a type of heart disease
  • Patellar luxation, which affects the kneecap
  • Hereditary myopathy, a condition affecting muscle function
  • Skin and coat problems
  • Dental disease

Regular veterinary checkups and responsible breeding practices can help identify and manage health concerns. Ask a veterinarian or responsible breeder about appropriate health screening.

Intelligence and Training

Devon Rex cats are intelligent and curious, making them well suited to interactive play and reward-based training.

With patience and positive reinforcement, they may learn simple tricks, respond to their names, or play games such as fetch.

Short, enjoyable training sessions help provide mental stimulation and strengthen their bond with their owners.

As a Family Cat

Devon Rex cats can make affectionate, playful, and entertaining family companions.

They generally enjoy human interaction and often like being involved in their owners' daily activities.

Because they are energetic and sociable, they benefit from homes where they receive plenty of attention, play, and mental stimulation.

Children should be taught to handle cats gently and respect their need for space and rest.

Lifespan

Devon Rex cats commonly live around 9–15 years, although some may live longer with appropriate care.

Genetics, nutrition, living conditions, and veterinary care can all influence their lifespan.

Fun Facts

  • The Devon Rex originated in Devon, England.
  • The breed's development began with a curly-coated kitten named Kirlee.
  • The Devon Rex has a different coat mutation from the Cornish Rex.
  • Its exceptionally large ears contribute to its distinctive appearance.
  • The coat is short, fine, and naturally wavy or curly.
  • The breed is sometimes described as pixie-like because of its unusual facial features.
  • Devon Rex cats often enjoy interactive games and learning tricks.
